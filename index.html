Buying tips

Free-tool offers

  • Home Depot’s main Milwaukee offer is a free select tool when you buy a qualifying kit: the $199 XC starter, the $249 High Output 2-pack, the $299 High Output starter, or the $399 FUEL drill and driver kit. Pick the free tool you actually want first, then choose the kit. One free tool per transaction is the usual rule, and the $199 starter had already sold out once this season.
  • Home Depot also advertised a free Forge 8.0Ah battery with select M18 tools, and a free second Forge pack with the dual-bay super charger kit.
  • Ace’s offer, as reported in late November, is the opposite shape: buy the $229 Forge 8.0Ah battery and choose a free M18 FUEL tool. Confirm it at your local Ace, because those stores can set their own promotions.
  • Some M12 batteries were listed as free with a select bare tool. If you already own M12 packs, the bare-tool prices at Walmart may be enough on their own.

Battery bundle math

  • Compare the pack price with what the same batteries cost in a starter kit, and with any free battery attached to a tool. The M12 XC 4.0Ah 2-pack at $79 is about $40 each, which is hard to beat if you just need more runtime.
  • The M18 XC 3.0Ah 2-pack at $79 is a cheap way to add smaller packs. The $199 XC 5.0Ah starter (when it is in stock) and the $349 Forge 8.0Ah 2-pack with the super charger are the better buys if you run higher-draw tools.
  • M12 and M18 batteries do not swap. Stay on the platform the person already owns, or start with a kit that includes batteries and a charger.
  • A “was” price on a kit is often the sum of the tools sold separately, not what the kit usually sells for. Use it as a ceiling, not as a typical street price.

Price matching at Home Depot and Lowe’s

  • Home Depot will often match a regular sale price from a qualifying competitor, including Lowe’s, Menards, Ace, Tractor Supply, and Amazon when Amazon itself is the seller, and it offers a 30-day post-purchase adjustment. The official policy is here: homedepot.com/c/price-match-and-price-check
  • Limited-time promotional events are typically excluded. That includes Black Friday doorbusters, holiday-only pricing, clearance, open-box, marketplace sellers, and most bundle or free-tool offers. They may match a tool’s price without giving you the other store’s free battery or free tool.
  • Lowe’s Lowest Price Guarantee has similar holes. Coverage of the official terms, including an Oct 19, 2026 archive, says Black Friday and buy-one-get-one offers are excluded, along with clearance, seasonal, closeout, open-box, custom, rebate, member, and marketplace prices. The official page is lowes.com/l/about/lowest-price-guarantee
  • The practical advice for this weekend is to buy the price you want at the store that has it, rather than counting on a match later. If a qualifying non-promotional price appears within 30 days, Home Depot’s adjustment window may still help.
